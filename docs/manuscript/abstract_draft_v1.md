**Title:** Beyond Fidelity: Evaluating the Validity of Explainable AI in Emergency Department Triage and Disposition — A Scoping Review

**Background:** Explainable AI (XAI) systems are increasingly proposed for emergency department (ED) intake, triage, and disposition decisions, on the premise that explanations help clinicians understand and appropriately trust model outputs under severe time and cognitive constraints. Regulatory frameworks, including FDA AI/ML-based Software as a Medical Device guidance and EU AI Act Article 13, are beginning to specify what evidence such interpretability claims must be backed by. Whether current EM-specific XAI research produces that evidence has not previously been reviewed.

**Objective:** To characterize which post-hoc explanation methods are deployed in EM clinical XAI studies and how their selection is justified (RQ1); how these studies evaluate explanation effectiveness across levels of human involvement (RQ2); and what evidence they provide toward the interpretability-validation expectations emerging in regulatory guidance (RQ3).

**Methods:** A JBI-methodology scoping review, reported per PRISMA-ScR, searched PubMed, IEEE Xplore, and ACM Digital Library (2015 to search execution; Embase excluded for lack of institutional access). Of 650 deduplicated records, 64 were forwarded to full-text screening; 7 met eligibility criteria after 55 were assessed (9 unretrievable). Each was extracted against a 43-column schema mapping method, justification type, a six-level evaluation taxonomy, ecological validity, two independent evaluation-tier frameworks, and four regulatory-relevant-evidence indicators.

**Results:** Rule extraction, SHAP, and LIME each appeared in 2-3 of the 7 included studies; 43% gave no stated justification for method selection. Six of seven studies evaluated their explanation using computational proxy metrics alone, involving no human participant; none reached application-grounded evaluation. Zero of seven studies met any of the four regulatory-relevant-evidence criteria assessed.

**Conclusions:** Current EM-specific XAI evaluation practice does not yet produce evidence that would satisfy emerging regulatory interpretability standards. This review is intended as scaffolding for the clinician-in-the-loop evaluation work this literature currently lacks.

**Keywords:** explainable artificial intelligence; emergency medicine; clinical decision support; interpretability; trust calibration; scoping review; regulatory evidence
